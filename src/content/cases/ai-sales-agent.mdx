<Section id="problem" title="Buyers wrote at night, and nobody answered">

VinFast's O2O sales page runs Messenger ads for electric scooters and cars, but no one was assigned to reply. Most conversations started after office hours, peaking from 7 to 9 pm. The group's own chatbot was not due until the end of November, so many of the conversations the ads paid for got no answer.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Buyers ask about money first, and the agent is only as good as its files">

- **Price questions dominate.** In the first 2,000 conversations, buyers asked about price, the monthly battery rental fee and installments far more than about specs.
- **I control the knowledge, not the model.** Meta's agent looks up the files I give it, then writes the reply. When an answer is wrong, the fix is almost always in those files or in its instructions.

<Section id="decisions" title="A grounded agent with one job and a way out to a person">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Launch Meta's agent now, as a stopgap">

I chose Meta's Business Agent over fixed keyword replies, which cannot handle open questions, and over waiting for the group chatbot. The trade-off: leads do not flow into the sales system on their own, so they need a manual hand-off.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Give it one job">

Answer product, price and policy questions, then guide the buyer to a consultation or an online deposit. Complaints, defects and refunds go to the hotline.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Ground every price and link">

Prices and links come only from the knowledge files, copied word for word. The agent never works out an on-road price by itself.

</Decision>
<Decision title="When it does not know, hand off">

The agent asks for a phone number once instead of guessing, and a person follows up.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="Live on 3 November, six days after the first setup">

Three smaller fixes followed in the first days. Buyers asking where to see a bike got the head-office address 49 times; the agent now answers from a list of 141 pickup points by province. Links now come only from the knowledge files, after seven links to a test site went out on launch day. Greetings now appear only in the first message.

<Section id="results" title="98% of messages answered, every fix measured, and a follow-up plan">

<Figure caption="Business Suite, 22 October to 18 November: 5,200 people messaged the page, mostly from ads. The 94% response rate and 1 minute 14 seconds to reply cover all 28 days, including the 12 before launch.">
  <Image src={insights} alt="Meta Business Suite messaging insights in Vietnamese: a 94% response rate and 1 minute 14 seconds response time, 5.2K total contacts, and a line chart that jumps from under 100 a day before November to between 200 and 560 a day after." widths={[600, 900, 1208]} sizes="(min-width: 1100px) 680px, 100vw" />
</Figure>

- **Reach:** 4,227 people messaged the page in the agent's first two weeks (3 to 17 November). In week one it answered 98.1% of messages by Meta's response rate; in the conversation export, half of its replies went out within 12 seconds.
- **Leads:** the agent now asks for a phone number when a buyer wants a quote, a test ride or a call back, so chats that used to go unanswered turn into leads sales can call.
- **Answer quality:** installment answers that named both ways to pay rose from 0% on launch day to between 84% and 93% a day from 5 November. The median reply got shorter, from 534 to 429 characters.
- **Regression test:** on 18 November an independent agent asked 23 test questions and 11 passed on the first run. The failures traced back to five knowledge entries that Meta had silently reverted. I re-created them, and the re-run passed except one answer about prepayment discounts, now on the fix list.

For the Zalo account, where an AI model would have needed a new data agreement, I chose rules over AI:

In a 478-message simulation, the rule-based design matched the right intent 94.1% of the time, up from 90.2% in the first draft; testers re-check it on live messages before launch.

For leads who leave a number but don't deposit, I scored the two email platforms on 18 criteria in six weighted groups. Marketing Cloud scored 74 of 100 against 40, and the team fixed it as the platform until the end of 2026. I planned four emails over nine days, each answering one buying question: the offer, a day with the scooter, the running costs and a test ride. The first is approved as the template.

<Section id="learned" title="The eval came from real conversations, not from my test cases">

- The worst failures were ones I would never have written as test cases: a head-office address for buyers asking where to see a bike, and a battery fee pushed to the hotline. Reading real conversations found them.
- On a platform I don't control, every change needs a re-test. Meta silently reverted edits to five knowledge entries twice.

</Section>
