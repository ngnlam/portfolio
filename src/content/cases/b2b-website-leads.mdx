<Section id="problem" title="No tracking, and enquiries buried in an inbox">

For a company selling robot training data, the website is where an AI lab first asks for a sample. The revamp brief put it plainly: no event tracking, and no record of what visitors did. Enquiries that did come in sat in an inbox, so BD heard about them late and couldn't tell a buyer from a job seeker.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Buyers read first, then ask for a sample">

1. **Buyers research before they talk.** B2B teams in AI and robotics read a lot before contacting anyone. Put the right offer where they are reading, a free sample, and they leave their details.
2. **"Saw the form" couldn't be measured.** There was no event for it, and the form sat at the bottom of every page, so scrolling down counted as seeing it.
3. **Leads arrived without context.** The form didn't ask what someone was interested in, and job applications came through the same form as B2B enquiries.
4. **News readers had no next step.** Articles ended without a call to action.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="A free sample, offered in four places">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="A sample, not a newsletter">

The default was a newsletter sign-up. I made a free sample dataset the main offer, because someone asking for data is telling you what they want to buy.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Four places to leave details, ranked">

P1: two buttons fixed in the menu, Get Sample Data and Talk to Our Team, plus a form at the end of the homepage and on each dataset page with the dataset already filled in. P2: an exit popup on desktop, shown once per session after 45 seconds and 30% of the page. P3: a chat bubble on product and Data Factory pages that opens after 90 seconds. Encord and Labelbox use the same menu pattern.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Fewer fields where intent is lower">

Two fields on the exit popup, four on the sample form, five on Talk to Our Team, where the person already wants a conversation.

</Decision>
<Decision title="One door, one lead event">

Samples only come through a form, so `generate_lead` is the single end point and every lead is counted once. Each form records where it sat and what the person is interested in.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Defer what isn't ready">

A resources hub (no content yet) and a humanoid product page (not on sale yet) moved to a later phase.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Track intent, nothing else">

The Data Factory spec has 13 events across discovery, evaluation and lead capture. I cut video plays, section scrolls and page-size changes.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="From sitemap to the Teams card">

- **Roadmap (April):** a brand asset with full tracking and an SEO base by June, steady organic growth through December, a sales touchpoint from 2027.
- **Sitemap and navigation** for the revamp, including which button leads where, and the design direction, worked out with the outside designer.
- **Growth element plan:** the four places to leave details on the site, plus off-site entry points: LinkedIn lead forms, a gated sample on Hugging Face, search ads and QR codes at events.
- **Tracking specs** for the new homepage and the Data Factory pages. I set up the homepage tags in Google Tag Manager myself.
- **Lead routing:** each enquiry posts to Microsoft Teams as a card with five buttons (B2B sales, recruiting, procurement, brand, unclear), and a Power Automate flow sends it on.
- **Sample email:** a download link arrives within minutes of a request. The vendor built it from my brief.
- **Weekly website report,** which I own, with Looker Studio dashboards fed from BigQuery. I also owned SEO for the site.

The new homepage went live on 27 June.

- Among people who started the form, completion rose from 50% to 71% after the redesign.
- Every enquiry reaches the right person in Teams in under a minute, so BD stopped sorting leads and compiling the spreadsheet by hand.
- The first B2B meeting request through the website came in mid-June and moved to an NDA the following week.
- After launch, tracking showed that 96% of people who opened the form never started it, which put the first fields at the top of the next round of fixes.
- Reports now leave out test traffic and job applications, so BD sees real enquiries.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="Fix the measurement before judging the redesign">

- Test traffic and job seekers made up a big share of early "leads", and any before and after built on them would have been noise.
- Tracking belongs in the first brief. The new homepage's own elements weren't in the vendor's original brief and had to be added later. Now I write the tracking spec alongside the sitemap.

</Section>
