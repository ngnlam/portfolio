<Section id="problem" title="No tracking, enquiries buried in an inbox, and data in four places">

For a company selling robot training data, the website is where an AI lab first asks for a sample. In April it had no event tracking, enquiries reached BD late, mixed with job applications, and ad and web data sat in separate tools with no shared definitions.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Buyers read first, then ask for a sample">

- **Buyers research before they talk.** The right offer, a free data sample, belongs where they read, and the only form sat at the bottom of every page.
- **Leads arrived without context.** No field said what someone wanted, and job seekers used the same form.
- **The early numbers were polluted.** In June, about 45% of the lead events recorded in analytics came from test and staging sites, so any before and after had to filter them out first.
- **AI labs ask assistants, not Google.** 68% of 266 target keywords had no search volume, so I made citations by AI assistants the search goal.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="A free sample, offered in five places, and every enquiry routed">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Offer a sample, not a newsletter">

A visitor who asks for a data sample has told us what they want to buy, so the sample request became the main call to action.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Five places, ranked by intent">

Two menu buttons and a form on every dataset page come first, then an exit popup, then a chat bubble. The lower the intent, the fewer the fields.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Track intent, nothing else">

I specified 13 events across discovery, evaluation and lead capture, and cut video plays, scrolls and page resizes.

</Decision>
<Decision title="One warehouse, every metric defined once">

As the only data person on the team, I built a four-layer warehouse in BigQuery that joins GA4, Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads and the lead tracker, so each metric is defined once and every report reads from the same place.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Send every enquiry to a person in Teams">

Each submission posts a card to Microsoft Teams, BD tags it in one tap, and a second flow routes it to sales, recruitment, procurement or brand. Our tenant blocked the "row changed" trigger, so the second flow runs every 15 minutes.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="results" title="One warehouse behind every report, and every enquiry routed to a person">

In the week after the June redesign, 5 of the 7 people who started the contact form finished it, against 50% the week before. The sample is too small to call a result, so I read it as a direction.

- Rebuilding the channel rules in the warehouse moved 589 paid LinkedIn sessions out of the wrong channel.
- A speed audit I ran traced slow mobile pages to reCAPTCHA loading three times; after the vendor's first fixes, the mobile PageSpeed lab score went from 27 to 49 (4 to 10 August). The rest of the fix list was still open when I left.
- **Next:** grow enquiry volume, then measure form completion again once enough people start the form.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="Fix the measurement before judging the redesign">

- Test traffic and job seekers made up a large share of the early leads. Any before and after built on them would have been noise.
- Tracking and speed belong in the first brief. I now write the tracking spec alongside the sitemap.

</Section>
