<Section id="problem" title="A wish list, not a requirement">

Dyno sees through cameras and a perception model. Engineering needed to know which tasks to build first, what the model must detect, and who acts on an alert. With a vague brief, months of perception work could go into the wrong thing.

<Section id="found" title="The real job happens on a scooter, at walking pace">

- **The round:** a guard rides about 90 minutes on a scooter, covering more than a thousand spaces, then rewinds camera footage at the desk. Dyno's value is alerting during the round.
- **The hard limit:** the battery lasts about two hours. That one fact decides the operating model.
- **Three questions decide an object's fate:** does it belong to someone, can one person carry it, is it hazardous.
- **The job ends at the fix:** guards hand off with a deadline and check back about 15 minutes later, a step a robot that only sends alerts would miss.
- **A cheaper signal:** the charger's LED color shows a charging violation with no integration.

<Section id="decisions" title="Four calls that shaped the spec">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Describe today's job before the robot's">

Every BRD opens with how the work is done now. I turned this into the template for every use case.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Keep only stories that change the build">

I cut 18 user stories to 13, then to 6, removing any story that would not change what engineering builds. The rest follow the life of an alert, from detection to closing the case.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Detect and alert, then let a person decide">

The robot reports and the guard decides. I ruled out four requests from the brief that would have let Dyno act on its own.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Label targets as assumptions">

Detection rates and a 10-second alert time are marked as assumptions to confirm with the client, so engineering does not read them as promises.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="Two BRDs, one spec, one shared template">

I delivered two BRDs, for parking violations and for trash or flammable items, and a parking spec that engineering could test against. The field work behind them: 72 minutes of recorded interviews with security staff and 54 site photos.

<Section id="results" title="Handed to engineering, partly built">

- I handed the requirements over at the end of August, when I moved to VinFast.

- **Next:** confirm detection accuracy on site against the targets in the spec.

<Section id="learned" title="One site visit changed the spec more than any meeting">

- The scooter round, the evidence rule and the charger lights came from one visit and changed the requirement more than any meeting.
- A requirement is finished when an engineer can test it, so every story ends in acceptance criteria.

</Section>
