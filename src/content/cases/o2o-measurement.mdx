<Section id="problem" title="The team could not tell which page or button led to a car deposit">

VinFast's O2O team sells cars and scooters online. In September the site had more tags than anyone owned, ad platforms saw only part of the leads and none of the car deposits, and my manager rebuilt the daily lead and deposit report by hand. Decisions rested on numbers nobody could trace back.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Tags nobody owned, and a deposit trail that broke at the shop">

- **The tag container had grown wild.** 212 tags, 81 of them paused, and lead tags on only 4 of 14 car product pages.
- **GA4 had nine things working, eight broken and six missing,** from deposit events to the link between the main site and the shop.
- **Deposits lost their source at checkout.** The payment step sends buyers through a payment gateway, and some deposits arrived in GA4 credited to the gateway instead of the ad that brought them.
- **Buyers deposit from the homepage, not from product pages.** 49% of sessions with a car deposit started on the homepage and only 10% on a product page, yet the homepage sent no button events at all.

<Section id="decisions" title="One event plan, checks built into every number">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="One event plan for every product page">

Not a fix per page. One plan sets the lead and deposit events for every car and scooter page, and since 30 October every button also reports its position and car model.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Audit what exists before proposing anything">

For the deposit question I had AI agents read the live tag container and the shop's checkout code and pull GA4. A second agent re-pulled the key numbers a different way; the totals matched, and the check exposed a connector bug that returns wrong numbers with "contains" filters.

</Decision>
<Decision title="A report that checks itself">

Every run checks that valid orders equal the sum of their four sources and that overlapping days match the previous report, so a wrong number stops the report instead of reaching my manager.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Keep the last good number">

When a source drops out or a day's GA4 count falls below 30% of normal, the dashboard keeps the last good number instead of showing a false drop.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

</Section>

<Section id="shipped" title="Tags on every page, an audit, a daily report and a dashboard">

- **Tracking:** lead tags on all 14 car and all 21 scooter product pages, car deposits reporting back to the ad account, and position tags on every button.
- **Deposit audit:** eight proposals from the AI-agent audit, and a two-item order to the team for the site.
- **Daily lead report:** an eight-sheet workbook run by one command in about 20 seconds, with its logic written down in 20 sections so a person or an AI agent can rebuild it.
- **Dashboard:** Meta Ads, Google Ads and GA4 in one place by car model, refreshed every morning, with a guide on how far to trust each number. It is on its fifth version.

- **Accuracy:** lead totals by page in the dashboard match the web total at 99.97% in a test run, and Meta numbers sit within 0.5% of the connector's pull.
- **What the data changed:** the position tags made the 26-day read of the car pages possible, and the report showed that what looked like retail orders doubling was a handful of bulk orders.
- **Next:** join leads to deposits in the sales system, so every channel is judged on deposits, not leads.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="The daily report stops itself if its totals do not add up">

- Writing the report logic down in 20 sections means anyone, or an AI agent, can rebuild it without me.
- Splitting the audit between agents, with one agent re-pulling the numbers blind, caught a connector bug a single pass would have missed.

</Section>
