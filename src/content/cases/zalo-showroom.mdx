<Section id="problem" title="Customers live in Zalo. The showroom lived on the website.">

In August the company approved a sales channel inside Zalo: browse cars and scooters, see each model, ask for a consultation. In September I was made business owner. Every lead had to land in the same sales system as every other channel, and be counted as Zalo.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Zalo leads were hiding under Facebook">

1. **Zalo had no source of its own.** Checking the sales system showed more than a thousand leads since 2025 logged as Facebook that had really come from Zalo. So a dedicated lead source went into the requirements in the first week.
2. **A deposit button changes the rules.** Legal advised that taking deposits online would bring the full e-commerce ordering rules into play.
3. **The build drifted from the website.** My audit against the website found most car prices out of date, missing colors and specs, and a model that was no longer on sale. A second audit of 17 scooter screens in Figma found missing, extra and duplicated models.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="Lead capture first, deposits later">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Phase 1 is four screens">

Home, a listing, a page per model and a consultation form. Deposits, e-contracts and test-drive booking moved to phase 2: a new sales system was due in October, so a deposit flow built now would be built twice.

</Decision>
<Decision title="The form lives inside the app">

Sending people to the website meant a frame inside a frame, so the form was built into the app.

</Decision>
<Decision title="A lead source of its own">

Every lead reaches the sales system tagged as Zalo Mini App, with UTM source and campaign fields. I ruled out writing campaign names into the subject field, because it already held three kinds of content and marks records the system creates. I proposed the same UTM fields for every channel.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Collect only what's needed">

The app reads only the Zalo user ID and sends the form straight to VinFast's servers. That's why the data protection officer agreed no data processing agreement with Zalo was needed.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

</Section>

<Section id="shipped" title="Four screens, 30 models, one form">

- Home with cars and scooters; cars split into personal and service vehicles, scooters into premium, mid-range and everyday; a page per model with colors, price and specs; a Get a consultation button at the bottom of every screen.
- Tracking requirements in the BRD, and the mini app set up on Zalo's developer portal.
- Content and design audits, with fix lists for the vendor.
- A mapping of 81 product codes so Salesforce and the sales system recognize every model.
- A demo screen for a new scooter, Feliz 2027, for the vendor's designer to follow.
- The acceptance checklist: 30 models, each checked for images and colors, price and specs, and a lead reaching the sales system; plus 5 checks on the form.

<Section id="results" title="95 checks passed. Launch is next.">

- All 95 acceptance checks passed in late October, and the growth manager signed off.
- Testing caught the form accepting only two models, one car and one scooter: the app had reused an outdated model list from an older app. The 81-code mapping fixed it before sign-off.
- The vendor is doing its final flow review before the app goes live. From day one, every Zalo lead will be countable on its own.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="The risky part was the data we reused">

- The form broke because of a model list copied from an older app, not because of new code. Now I check reused data first.
- Agreeing the lead source in week one meant the measurement was ready before the screens were.

</Section>
