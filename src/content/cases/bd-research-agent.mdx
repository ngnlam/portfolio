<Section id="problem" title="Research by hand, and enquiries buried in an inbox">

VinDynamics sells robot training data to AI labs. BD found every prospect by hand, from the reason to reach out to the message itself. The website, where a lab first asks for a sample, had no event tracking, and enquiries reached BD late, mixed with job applications.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Timing beats a company list, and buyers read before they talk">

- **Signals say when to reach out.** A product launch, a regulatory approval, ML hiring or a funding round tells you a lab needs data now. I defined eight signal types.
- **Leads came with names, not emails,** and Apollo's paid plan was too expensive for a pilot, so contact lookup had to be rationed.
- **Buyers research before they talk.** The right offer, a free data sample, belongs where they read, and the only form sat at the bottom of every page.
- **AI labs ask assistants, not Google.** 68% of 266 target keywords had no search volume, so I made citations by AI assistants the search goal.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="Score before spending, keep a person on the send button, and route every enquiry">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Signals, not a static list">

The agent reads funding news, launches, approvals and research papers, plus a watchlist of 28 target companies, so every lead arrives with a reason to talk now.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Score before paying for a contact lookup">

Claude scores each company from 1 to 10, and only companies scoring 8 or more go on to the paid contact lookup. The lookup runs as a separate step, so a used-up quota never stops the research.

</Decision>
<Decision title="The agent drafts, a person sends">

I ruled out automated sending and LinkedIn automation to protect the team's accounts. The agent drafts each message; BD reviews and sends it.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Offer a free sample in five places, ranked by intent">

Two menu buttons and a form on every dataset page come first, then an exit popup, then a chat bubble. The lower the intent, the fewer the fields.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Send every enquiry to a person in Teams">

Each form submission posts a card to Microsoft Teams, BD tags it in one tap, and a second flow routes it to sales, recruitment, procurement or brand.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="A morning digest for BD, a new website, and a Teams card for every enquiry">

- **Research agent (May):** a 9 am digest in Teams of up to 20 researched companies, each with the buying signal, the people to contact and a draft message, plus Qualified and Not a fit buttons.
- **Document fact-checker:** BD uploads a PDF such as a press release or a proposal, and gets a verdict, a score, the claims checked and the concerns, in a sheet and a Teams card.
- **Website (live 27 June):** built on my sitemap, tracking spec and lead capture plan, with 13 tracked events across discovery, evaluation and lead capture. I set up the homepage tags myself.

<Section id="results" title="52 prospects in four days, all contacted, and seven in ten form starters now finish">

- **Research agent:** from 12 to 15 May it delivered 52 researched companies across 5 sectors and 9 countries, and BD contacted all 52. In the June review, BD valued the research context more than the drafts, so the next version leads with context.
- **Website:** in mid-June the contact form brought the first B2B meeting request through the site, and it moved to an NDA the following week.

<Section id="learned" title="Measure honestly before claiming a win">

- Early drafts of this case said 100% fit and 90% qualified. Those were review criteria, not results, so I took them out.
- Test traffic and job seekers made up a large share of the site's early leads. I filtered them out before judging the redesign.
- Every automated step now has a cap on how often it runs, after one step ran every 30 minutes instead of daily and used up the month's quota.

</Section>
