<Section id="problem" title="Four jobs a small team did by hand">

VinDynamics sells robot training data to AI labs in the US, Japan, Korea and the UAE. Four jobs took the team's time: BD researched every prospect by hand, leaders sent documents such as press releases and proposals back for factual fixes, the Brand team wrote each press release from scratch, and nobody watched what target companies posted on LinkedIn.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Timing beats a company list, and the platform limits decide the design">

- **Signals say when to reach out.** A product launch, a regulatory approval, ML hiring or a funding round tells you a lab needs data now, so the agent starts from news, not from a list.
- **Contacts were the expensive step.** Apollo's free plan returned no contacts, and its paid plan was not worth it for a pilot, so contact lookup had to be saved for the best prospects.
- **The company network sets the rules.** Our Microsoft 365 tenant blocked some Power Automate triggers, the group network blocked Google Drive and Google scripts, and n8n's starter plan allows 2,500 runs a month. Every design had to fit inside those limits.

</Section>

<Section id="decisions" title="Cheap filters first, a person on every final step">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Start from signals, not a static list">

The agent reads more than 20 live sources, such as approvals, research papers and startup news, plus a watchlist of 28 target labs, so every prospect arrives with a reason to talk now.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Use a cheap model to filter, a strong one to write">

Claude Haiku scores each signal from 1 to 10 and drops weak ones. Claude Sonnet researches only the survivors and drafts the messages, and contact lookup is saved for the top-scoring prospects. I turned down Apollo's paid plan.

</Decision>
<Decision title="The agent drafts, a person sends">

I ruled out automated LinkedIn sending tools because of the risk of account bans. Each morning at 9 BD gets the top 20 prospects as Teams cards with the reason, the people to contact and a draft; BD reviews and sends.

</Decision>
<Decision title="One model call per document, not sixty">

The first fact-checker checked each claim separately, about 60 calls per document. The second makes one Claude call with web search that returns a verdict, a score and the concerns, and it runs in 79 seconds on an 8-page test file.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Host the press release writer where it can actually run">

I compared seven ways to give the Brand team a shared tool and shipped a Cloudflare Worker that keeps the API key secret.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="Four tools, each ending with a person">

<Figure caption="How the research agent works, recreated as a diagram: from a buying signal to a morning card that BD reviews.">
  <Pipeline />
</Figure>

- **Research agent (May):** n8n finds signals, Claude filters, researches and drafts, Power Automate posts the morning cards to Teams and records every click in the BD pipeline file. An evening card asks BD to log replies.
- **Document fact-checker:** BD uploads a PDF through a Microsoft Form; the card that comes back gives a verdict, a score, the top concerns and links to sheets with every claim checked.
- **Press release writer:** a one-page form for the Brand team that drafts a release in the group's house style, trained on the group's own published releases.
- **LinkedIn monitor:** watches a list of key people at target companies and posts each new post to a Teams chat with the BD owner tagged. Running from 14 May.

<Section id="learned" title="Check the platform's limits before designing">

- One step ran every 30 minutes instead of daily and used up the month's n8n runs. Every automated step now gets a run budget before it goes live.
- Blocked triggers, blocked file hosting and one personal AI account shaped every choice. I now list the platform's limits first, then design.
- Early drafts of this case said 100% fit and 90% qualified. Those were review criteria, not results, so I took them out.

</Section>
