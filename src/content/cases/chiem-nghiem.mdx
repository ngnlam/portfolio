<Section id="problem" title="People read their chart when they're stuck, and most sites make it worse">

Tử vi is what many people in Vietnam open when work feels uncertain. Popular sites quote dramatic one-liners, never show why, and sell certainty. I wanted to build one product end to end, solo, for that moment.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="The leading competitor had already built my first idea">

- **Readings change feelings, not decisions.** Everyone I interviewed described months of indecision; talking it through or taking one small step helped most.
- **People don't type their real question.** In a concierge pilot, where I gave a reading by hand, the question asked was not the one that mattered, and the reading changed nothing the person did.
- **Working people didn't ask for AI.** In the working-people sample, none of 100 Facebook group posts or 341 TikTok comments mentioned it.
- **The obvious MVP existed.** My teardown of five competitors showed the leader already did roughly my first idea.

<Section id="decisions" title="One job, every score explained, nothing generated at runtime">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Build now">

Not a five-person prototype test. Whether people come back on their own only shows on a live product, and AI made building cheap.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Show the reason behind every score">

A 1 to 5 scale with one tap to "why". Not scores like 73/100, which look precise and aren't.

</Decision>
<Decision title="No AI at runtime">

Readings come from more than 1,700 rules taken from one reference book: no cost per visit, birth data stays on the phone, nothing invented. The trade-off is less fluent text.

</Decision>
<Decision title="A website, not an app">

Apple is tightening its rules on fortune-telling apps; a website shipped the same week.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Overrule the AI where it matters">

Twelve palaces, not the seven Claude proposed, because thoroughness earns trust here. I cut accounts, payments, AI chat and numerology (no demand signal).

</Decision>
</Decisions>

</Section>

<Section id="results" title="Live, and measuring the one behavior that matters">

- **Metric:** the share of early users who come back on their own on two different days within 14. 40% or more means continue, under 40% means fix, and none means rethink.
- **Guardrail:** if two people say a reading made them more anxious, I stop and rewrite the tone.
- **Next:** the first read of returning users in late November, which this page will show.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="Claude wrote most of the code, so I spent my time testing it">

- The competitor teardown was in my plan and got skipped until I caught it. It changed the positioning, so it is now a fixed step before design.
- With AI, the bottleneck moved from writing code to verifying it. Without an independent check, 35% of the sampled sentences would have shipped attached to the wrong chart.

</Section>
