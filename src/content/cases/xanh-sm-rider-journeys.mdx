<Section id="problem" title="One message calendar for millions of riders">

Xanh SM, Vietnam's electric ride-hailing app, had 2.1 million riders who had signed up but never taken a trip, and 1.1 million who had ridden and then stopped. Messaging ran on a calendar: the same push to everyone on the same day, judged by click-through rate. The journeys that did exist ran in isolation, so nobody could see the whole system.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Riders who never rode are not one group, and clicks hide the result">

- **Never-ridden riders differ by intent.** Some never opened the app after signing up; some opened it and checked prices; some requested a ride that did not happen. Each needs a different nudge, and the first group gives no signal at all.
- **Lapsed riders differ by habit.** A rider who used to ride often and stopped 21 days ago is a different case from an occasional rider gone for 28 days.
- **Clicks hid the real result.** A message could win on click-through and change no trips, so trips became the measure.

<Section id="decisions" title="Behavior in, trips out">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Enter journeys by behavior, not by calendar">

Each rider enters the journey that matches their last action, instead of receiving whatever the calendar sends that day.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Leave out riders with no signal">

Riders who never opened the app got no journey: there was nothing to respond to, and offers to them would have been spent blind.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Create a first trip, don't borrow one">

I turned down booking a first trip on a new rider's behalf, because it only moves demand from one person to another instead of creating it.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Judge every journey on trips">

Weekly A/B tests were read on trips completed, not on opens or clicks, and a journey's sending frequency went up or down based on that.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Fixed send times, approved offers, no guilt">

Messages go out at 8 am, 11 am or 7 pm. Offers come only from the approved matrix for each of four market groups. Lapsed riders never hear "we miss you"; they hear what the deal is.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

</Section>

<Section id="shipped" title="30+ journeys, one map, a weekly report">

- **Journeys in CleverTap:** more than 30 across rider groups, provinces and services (bike, car, express and food), including 14 for never-ridden and lapsed riders.
- **New markets:** journeys for riders arriving in and leaving tourist provinces, and three journeys built with the local team in the Philippines.
- **A weekly report** on open rate, click-through and trips for every journey, which set the next week's changes.
- **Tone rules and a prompt template** so AI tools could draft push and Zalo notification messages in the team's voice.

- First trips by new riders rose 28% and churn fell 12%, month over month.
- Every key rider group now has an automated journey with clear entry and exit rules, mapped by province and service.
- **How to read the 1.7 million:** these are trips CleverTap attributes to riders while they were in a journey, not all caused by it. That is why each journey was also judged in weekly A/B tests on trips.

</Section>

<Section id="learned" title="Splitting riders by behavior made the whole system visible">

- Splitting riders by their last action turned scattered journeys into one system, mapped by province and service.
- Writing the tone rules down, with a prompt template, let AI tools draft in the team's voice without me rewriting every message.

</Section>
