<Section id="problem" title="A new design, and the lead pop-up was gone">

In late September VinFast's car pages moved to a new domain and design, and the lead pop-up went with them. Leads per visit fell sharply. The only form sat at the very end of a long page, and a shopper who wanted the on-road cost or a monthly payment had to leave to find it.

<Section id="found" title="No button opened the form, and the shop's own data was stale">

- **Every button led away.** I mapped every button on the nine car pages live at the time: most sent people to the shop or only scrolled down, and the only form sat at the very end.
- **The cost data was out of date.** The shop still listed 63 provinces and interest rates last updated in November 2023.
- **The new buying pages lost people in seconds.** On mobile, 42% of views lasted under three seconds, and in 34 session recordings nobody tapped Get a consultation.
- **The domain move hid a search problem.** Every moved page passed, but canonical tags on most car pages pointed to broken URLs.

<Section id="decisions" title="Entry points first, then cost, then traffic">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Ship the entry points first">

New entry points could ship in weeks; reworking every section would take months. I shipped the entry points first and made the section rework and the cost tool their own proposal.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Keep the deposit button in the hero">

The old hero had deposit and test drive side by side; the new hero has only the deposit button. When switching it to a consultation button came up, I argued to keep deposit: in its first week, people who tapped it reached the payment step 4x as often as mid-page tappers (5.1% against 1.3%), mostly returning visitors. The hero drew 37% of button taps.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Open the form where interest peaks">

The button after the photos used to scroll to the bottom. Now it opens the form in place, and I kept my sticky bar small and added the UI designer's header button that opens the form.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Tag every entry point">

Every button carries a position tag instead of sharing one generic lead event. That tag is how I can show where each lead started.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Let buyers enter their own rate">

With the shop's rates two years old, the cost tool asks buyers for their own interest rate instead of showing a wrong one.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Argue for fixes before more ad spend">

The plan was to send more ads to the two new buying pages. I argued the pages should be fixed first and ranked 17 changes into fix now, round one and round two. The director approved all 17; the ad plan went ahead in parallel.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="shipped" title="New entry points on the car pages, a cost tool and a checked domain move">

- **Entry points:** the mid-page form went live on 23 October; the sticky bar, the header button and the position tags followed on 30 October.
- **Cost tool:** approved by the division director on 2 November. The mid-page form is live and the fuel-savings comparison has been in user testing since 13 November; on-road cost and installments wait on an API from the shop team.
- **Buying pages:** a 17-row current-versus-proposed spec, approved by the division director on 18 November, with about 2.8 leads per 1,000 sessions as the baseline to beat.
- **Domain move:** 30 product pages, 9 car and 21 scooter, checked at the server, search and analytics level, plus a technical SEO fix list for the canonical errors.

<Section id="results" title="Four in five car-page leads came through the new entry points in the first month">

Over the first 26 days, 87% of car-page leads started at one of the new entry points, up from 54% in the first nine days: 81% through the mid-page form and sticky bar in my spec, the rest through the header button the UI designer added. Part of the rise comes from how leads are counted, since from about 1 November the old bottom form opened from the sticky bar and header button instead of sitting on the page. Scooter pages, which did not change, stayed flat over the same weeks, at about 0.6 leads per 1,000 views before and after. The 81% and 87% are shares of car-page leads, not a lift in lead volume.

<Section id="learned" title="A tag on every button paid for itself">

- Tagging every position cost almost nothing and turned a 26-day re-measure into a few minutes of work.
- Forms that open on request finish more often than a form placed on the page: 75% from the sticky bar against 47% mid-page, 30 October to 11 November. Making every entry point open the form is my next test.

</Section>
