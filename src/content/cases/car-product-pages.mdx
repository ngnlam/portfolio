<Section id="problem" title="A new design, and the lead pop-up was gone">

In late September VinFast moved its car product pages to a new domain with a new design, and the lead pop-up went away with it. Visitors kept coming, but leads per visit dropped sharply. For a car brand, those leads are the start of the sales pipeline.

</Section>

<Section id="found" title="Ten buttons a page, and none of them opened the form">

People still came to research a car, scrolled, and left. I mapped every button on every car page: ten per page. Seven sent people to the online shop to place a deposit, and two only scrolled them to the bottom. The only way to leave contact details was the form at the very end, so anyone who made up their mind halfway down had to scroll all the way or leave.

<Section id="decisions" title="Entry points now, the section rework later">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Entry points first">

Reworking every section would take months. New ways to reach the form could ship in weeks, so I went with entry points first, cars before scooters. The section rework became <a href="/work#cost-tool" class="ul">its own proposal</a>.

</Decision>
<Decision title="One button in the hero">

Deposit and test drive competed for people who had just arrived. I put the starting price up top with one deposit button. People who are ready press it, everyone else keeps reading, and test drive still lives in the header.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Open the form in place">

A consultation button already sat right after the photos of the car, where interest peaks, but it scrolled people to the bottom. I specified it to open the form where they are.

</Decision>
<Decision title="A header button, from the designer">

I proposed a sticky bar that appears after the hero. The UI designer pointed out that too many fixed elements would crowd the page and suggested the header's test-drive button open the consultation form too. That became the third entry point.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Three required fields">

Research favors fewer fields, but the CRM can't create a lead without a name, phone number and email. I kept all three and wrote the trade-off down.

</Decision>
<Decision title="A tag on every entry point">

The default was one generic lead event. I gave every entry point its own position tag, which is how I can show exactly where leads now start.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="learned" title="Small changes in the right place come first">

- A tag on every entry point costs almost nothing and pays off on day one. It's why I can show where half the leads now start.
- Four ways to reach the form shipped in weeks; the section rework would have taken months.
- Next: the mid-page cost tool and form I've already prototyped, so more of the people who read the page leave their details.

</Section>
