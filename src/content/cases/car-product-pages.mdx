<Section id="problem" title="A new design, and the lead pop-up was gone">

In late September the car pages moved to a new domain and design, and the lead pop-up went with them. Visits held, but leads per visit fell sharply.

<Section id="found" title="Ten buttons a page, and none of them opened the form">

I mapped every button on 14 car pages: ten per page. Seven sent people away to the shop, two only scrolled down, and the tenth was the submit button of the only form, at the very end. Anyone who decided halfway had to scroll or leave.

<Section id="decisions" title="Entry points now, the section rework later">

<Decisions>
<Decision title="Ship the entry points first">

New entry points could ship in weeks; reworking every section would take months. I shipped the entry points first and turned the section rework into <a href="/work#cost-tool" class="ul">its own proposal</a>.

</Decision>
<Decision title="One button in the hero">

The old hero had deposit and test drive side by side; I kept one deposit button. Two weeks after launch the hero drew 37% of button taps, and 5.1% of the people who tapped it reached payment, against 1.3% for the mid-page button.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Open the form where interest peaks">

The button after the photos used to scroll to the bottom. Now it opens the form in place.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Use the designer's header button">

I kept my sticky bar small so it would not crowd the page, and added the UI designer's header button, which opens the form, as the third new entry point.

</Decision>
<Decision title="Tag every entry point">

Every button carries a position tag instead of sharing one generic lead event. That tag is how I can show where each lead started.

</Decision>
</Decisions>

<Section id="results" title="Nearly nine in ten car-page leads now start at the new entry points">

Over the first 26 days, 87% of car-page leads started at one of the new entry points in my spec, up from 54% in the first nine days. Scooter pages, which did not change, stayed flat over the same weeks, at about 0.6 leads per 1,000 views before and after.

- **How I checked:** rerunning the first nine days reproduces the 54%, and lead totals per page match across two separate pulls. VF Wild, still on the old template, is left out.
- **Not proven yet:** whether these leads become deposits more often. That needs leads joined to deposits in the sales system, which is the next read.

<Section id="learned" title="A tag on every button paid for itself">

- Tagging every position cost almost nothing and turned a 26-day re-measure into a few minutes of work.
- Forms that open on request finish more often than a form placed on the page. Making every entry point open the form is my next test.

</Section>
